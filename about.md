# About Engineering AI Intelligence

Engineering AI is developing too quickly—and spans too many engineering disciplines—to justify a permanent classification system at the start of this project. The first goal is therefore simple: collect public information consistently, preserve direct links, and learn from the accumulated literature before deciding which themes deserve deeper research.

Each edition covers one complete calendar month. It reviews engineering software, industrial applications, original research, code and datasets, standards, projects, partnerships, and technical exchanges. Engineering modeling and simulation receive sustained attention, including CAD and CAE workflows, CFD, FEA, system simulation, surrogate models, optimization, solver integration, calibration, verification, validation, and test–simulation correlation.

Digital Twin is observed broadly across products, assets, processes, factories, vehicles, infrastructure, and other physical systems. Information that connects with physical modeling, vehicle or tire dynamics, simulation, validation, or engineering decision-making receives particular attention, without excluding unfamiliar fields.

The public monthly report follows a literature-review format. Each entry gives the publication date and organization, a concise description of what the source reports, and direct links. Detailed evidence assessments, candidate classifications, rejected materials, coverage logs, and follow-up questions remain in the private research record rather than appearing in the public article.
