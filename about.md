# About Engineering AI Intelligence

Engineering AI is developing too quickly—and spans too many engineering disciplines—to justify a permanent classification system at the start of this project. My first goal is therefore simpler: collect public information consistently, preserve the evidence, and learn from the material before deciding which themes deserve deeper research.

Each edition covers one complete calendar month. It looks across engineering software, industrial deployments, original research, code and datasets, standards, projects, partnerships, and technical exchange. AI-assisted research helps discover and organize the material; the reader-facing report is checked against direct sources and reviewed by a human.

## What receives extra attention

Engineering modeling and simulation receive sustained attention, including CAD and CAE workflows, CFD, FEA, system simulation, surrogate and reduced-order models, optimization, solver integration, calibration, verification, validation, and test–simulation correlation.

Digital Twin is monitored broadly across products, assets, processes, factories, vehicles, infrastructure, and other physical systems. The name alone is not treated as evidence. Reports distinguish the twinned object, lifecycle purpose, model and data connection, state-update mechanism, maturity, and validation when sources disclose them.

Information that connects materially with physical modeling, vehicle or tire dynamics, simulation, validation, or engineering decision-making is highlighted for reading value. That professional lens does not exclude unfamiliar fields.

## How to read the reports

Every core story states what changed, why it matters, the available evidence and maturity, a concrete next signal when one exists, and direct source links. Relevant but preliminary, historical, promotional, secondary, or outcome-free items appear separately as **Context / limited evidence**.

The monthly sequence is intentionally open. Temporary tags and story order help retrieval; they are not a claim that Engineering AI has already settled into fixed categories. Early research gaps are starting arguments, not a framework into which all later information must fit.

The public reports contain no private research notes, confidence scores, rejected candidates, or workflow records. Absence of a public signal does not prove inactivity.
